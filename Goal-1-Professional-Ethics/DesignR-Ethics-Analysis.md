# E1.4 — DesignR Ethical Case Analysis

**Evidence type:** Written analytical artefact, adapted from the student's draft A3 report dated **5 November 2026**.  
**Relevant A1 goal:** Professional Ethics and Indigenous Communities.  
**Verification boundary:** This page documents a case analysis. It **does not** verify completion of the Working Ethically or Indigenous Professional Capability Canvas modules, assessment scores, hours, or consultation with Indigenous communities.

## Scenario and central problem

In the hypothetical DesignR scenario, a company considers selling AI-generated imagery associated with Indigenous culture without directly involving relevant Indigenous artists or communities. Possible measures such as labelling the images as AI-generated, researching context and making donations do not by themselves establish community authority, consent or an agreement on commercial reuse.

## Analysis: beyond a technical or licensing checklist

- **Consequences:** A utilitarian perspective needs to consider harms to artists, communities and cultural representation alongside potential business benefits.
- **Rights and participation:** A rights-based perspective asks who may legitimately decide how cultural materials and representations are used.
- **Fairness:** Payment, attribution, benefit sharing and decision-making power matter; post-production donations are not a substitute for participation.
- **Professional accountability:** A software licence or an image's public visibility does not automatically settle whether use is culturally appropriate.

The [AIATSIS Code of Ethics for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Research](https://aiatsis.gov.au/sites/default/files/2020-10/aiatsis-code-ethics.pdf) identifies Indigenous self-determination, Indigenous leadership, impact and value, and sustainability and accountability. Its research principles can inform an ethical discussion of the hypothetical business scenario, but do not provide a one-size-fits-all legal decision.

## Design implications for software or AI

A responsible implementation process would need to clarify:
1. The purpose and origin of the data, imagery or representations.
2. Which individual or collective rights-holders should participate in decisions.
3. How uses, reuse, attribution, retention and benefits would be agreed and recorded.
4. How concerns, withdrawal requests and governance would be addressed, including disclosure of technical limits.

**Important limitation:** This analysis proposes requirements; it does **not** claim Indigenous consultation, consent, or approval occurred.

## Reflection grounded in the draft report

The central insight is that a technically feasible system may still be inappropriate to build without proper participation and authority. This reframes the question from **“Can we implement it?”** to **“Who should be involved in deciding whether and how to implement it?”**

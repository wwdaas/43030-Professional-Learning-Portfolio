## Concrete learning artefact — what the submitted email argues

The email acknowledges commercial pressure and existing measures (research, AI labelling, donations, and rights-managed licences), then explains why those measures are insufficient without direct Indigenous participation. It identifies specific questions concerning cultural authority, AI training-data provenance, commercial reuse, informed consent, attribution, and downstream modification of imagery.

## Four actual recommendations in the submitted email

1. **Pause new releases** and review listings, cultural claims, source materials, permissions and problematic images, using appropriate Indigenous cultural and legal advice.
2. **Hold paid consultations** with relevant Traditional Owners, artists and organisations before future products, seeking free, prior and informed consent and agreeing controls on use, attribution, adaptation and withdrawal.
3. **Commission and license original Indigenous artists' work** with negotiated fees, royalties and decision-making authority. Pursue unrelated stock-image products as a possible short-term commercial alternative.
4. **Establish an Indigenous Cultural and Intellectual Property policy** including Indigenous-led review, approvals, training, provenance checks, purchaser restrictions, complaint procedures and audit.

The closing proposes an initial management review and paid community consultation pilot ahead of any broader relaunch.
